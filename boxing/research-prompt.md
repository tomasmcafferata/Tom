# Boxer Style Analysis — Deep Research Prompt

Replace `[BOXER NAME]` and `[DIVISION]` before running.

---

## Prompt

You are an expert boxing analyst and coach. Produce a comprehensive, expert-level style analysis of **[BOXER NAME]**, **[DIVISION]** fighter. The audience is an intermediate-to-advanced competitive boxer who will use this document for both personal training and coaching others at mixed levels.

Write in a technical but readable tone — like a senior coach explaining to a serious student, not a sports journalist. Avoid generic praise. Be specific, mechanical, and instructive.

Structure the document exactly as follows:

---

## 1. Style matrix classification

Classify **[BOXER NAME]** using the three-dimension style matrix:

- Ring movement: Outboxer (O) or Pressure (P)
- Punching style: Sniper (S) or Volume (V)
- Attack dynamic: Starter (S) or Counter (C)

State the resulting code (e.g. O.V.C) and its archetype name. Then write 3–4 sentences explaining why each dimension fits, including any nuance or hybrid tendencies (e.g. shifts from Counter to Starter in specific tactical situations).

---

## 2. Fighter identity & philosophical core

Describe the fighter's philosophical core as a coherent system, not a list of traits. Identify the single governing principle that makes all their decisions legible — what are they actually trying to do in the ring at the deepest level? Name the mental model explicitly (e.g. "chaos as control," "geometry over violence," "attrition before execution"). Then explain how this philosophy was shaped by their lineage, trainer, and background — not just what school they came from, but what that school permitted or denied them developmentally. Close with: how does this philosophy manifest under maximum stress — when hurt, behind on cards, or facing an opponent who neutralizes their primary tool?

---

## 3. Physical profile & attribute requirements

List the fighter's stance, reach, height, and build relative to their division. Then, for each key physical attribute, explain the mechanical consequence for how the style functions — not just the fact itself:

- **Reach:** What distances does it open or close that a shorter fighter cannot access? What does that mean for their punching lanes and counter-exposure?
- **Chin/durability:** How does it change the risk calculus of their offensive choices — what do they attempt that a fragile fighter cannot?
- **Cardio:** At what point in the fight does the conditioning advantage become decisive, and what does the opponent feel when it kicks in?

Close with: which of these attributes are non-negotiable prerequisites for the style to function, and which are amplifiers that enhance but do not define it?

---

## 4. Footwork & distance management

Name the primary movement patterns and describe their mechanics. Explain how they enter and exit range. Describe how they manage center of gravity and balance during movement and combination.

If the fighter uses stance-switching, analyze it as a distinct tactical layer — not a footnote:

- When do they switch: as an entry mechanism, mid-combination, or as a reset?
- What does the switch accomplish tactically (angle change, lead-hand swap, power-side shift)?
- How polished is the execution — trained system or reactive improvisation?
- What does it expose during the transition moment?

Close with: what makes their positioning difficult to time, close, or escape from?

---

## 5. Punching mechanics & kinetic chain

For each primary punch, describe the mechanics as if explaining to someone watching it for the first time — use a physical analogy (a whip, a trebuchet, a pendulum) to make the force generation concrete. Then explain:

- What makes it land differently than it looks — why does the opponent not see it coming despite its unconventional trajectory?
- What is the setup condition — what has to be true about the opponent's position for this punch to work?
- What is the guard position during the throw — what defensive exposure does this punch create, and why does the fighter accept that trade?

Close with: what is the signature mechanical feature that distinguishes this fighter's punching from others of the same style type?

---

## 6. Offensive system

Document the primary weapons and their specific variations (minimum 3 variations per main weapon). For each core combination, describe the beat structure explicitly — not just the punch sequence, but the timing, pauses, and rhythm shifts between shots. Explain:

- How they set up the power shot
- How they use tempo shifts, broken rhythm, or change of speed to create openings
- How the combination architecture varied across different opponents or fights

Cite specific fights and rounds where each variation was deployed.

---

## 7. Defensive system

Identify the primary defense mechanism and explain how it functions. Describe how defense flows directly into offense in their system. Document specific counters deployed off defensive positions with fight references.

Address what they do when hurt or cornered — not just the general principle, but specific documented behavior from fights. Close with: what is the single condition that makes the defensive system fail, and which opponent archetype is most likely to create that condition?

---

## 8. What to absorb — and what to skip

**Universal principles (any boxer can steal these regardless of build or style):** Identify 2–3 core tactical or philosophical principles. For each, explain concretely how to begin applying it — a specific drill, a sparring focus, or a combination to work on.

**Attribute-dependent techniques (approach with caution):** Identify 2–3 techniques that require specific physical attributes to execute safely. For each, explain what goes wrong for a fighter who attempts it without those attributes.

**Accessibility spectrum:** Distinguish explicitly what is accessible at beginner level, what requires intermediate foundation, and what is advanced-only. This section will be read by fighters at mixed levels — make the distinctions clear.

---

## 9. Sparring cues & opponent archetypes

**Triggers to look for in live sparring:** Identify 3 specific moments or positional cues that signal when to apply this fighter's patterns. Ground each trigger in a documented fight example.

**Performance against matrix archetypes:** Analyze matchups against at least 3 different matrix types using the code format (e.g. O.V.C vs. P.S.S). For each, describe the dynamic, cite a real or hypothetical opponent, and explain why the matchup favors or disfavors this style structurally.

**Against southpaws specifically:** Analyze how the open-stance dynamic changes the geometry of this fighter's primary weapons. Does their lead hand advantage or disadvantage shift? Which core combinations break down against a southpaw guard, and which become more effective? Name a specific southpaw opponent if one exists in their record, or identify the southpaw matrix type that poses the greatest structural problem.

**Common replication mistakes:** The 3 most frequent errors fighters make when trying to apply this style in live work — and what to do instead.

---

## 10. Structural gaps & counter entry points

Identify 3 structural areas where the style breaks down. For each gap, name which matrix archetype exploits it most effectively and why. Close with 3 specific tactical adjustments an opponent can make to neutralize the core strengths — keep these concrete and actionable, not abstract. For each adjustment, cite a fight where it was successfully applied or partially attempted.

---

## Research instructions

- After each section, cite specific fights, rounds, or moments where relevant.
- Use CompuBox data or punch statistics where available.
- Prioritize primary sources: fight footage analysis, post-fight breakdowns by coaches, trainer interviews, fighter interviews.
- Search YouTube transcripts and boxing community discussions (Reddit, boxing forums) in addition to written articles — significant technical detail on punch mechanics and footwork lives in video breakdowns.
- If the fighter has multiple distinct career phases (early, prime, late), note which phase each section primarily describes and flag major stylistic changes across phases.
- If the fighter is less documented (regional, amateur, historical), triangulate from fighters of the same school, trainer, or national system.
